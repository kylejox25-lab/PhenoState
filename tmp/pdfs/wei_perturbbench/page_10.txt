In our study, the four baseline models and linearModel per -
formed relatively poorly, which differs from the findings of previous  
studies—potentially due to differences in data preprocessing and  
the size of the training set (Supplementary Note 18). In datasets 
with 
abundant fine-tuning data, deep learning and foundation 
models such as CPA and scGPT performed well, particularly on 
population-average metrics. For example, in the Replogle-RPE1es -
sential and Replogle-K562essential datasets 41, CPA achieved the  
highest accuracy in terms of both MSE and PCC-delta24 (Fig. 4b). Further 
analysis revealed that the performance advantage of CPA over train-
Mean increased as the similarity between the test and training pertur-
bations decreased (Fig. 4c). This suggests that deep learning models 
have superior generalizability when fine-tuned on sufficiently large 
datasets. T o further validate our conclusion, we conducted an experi-
ment where we varied the training (fine-tuning) set size (10%, 30%, 50% 
and 80%) on the Replogle-RPE1essential and Replogle-K562essential 
datasets, keeping all other parameters constant. The results showed 
a clear trend that the performance of scGPT improved with increasing 
amounts of fine-tuning data, whereas the trainMean baseline model 
remained stable or showed slight degradation (Extended Data Fig. 5).
For the combined-perturbation datasets, we focused on accuracy 
across three perturbation categories: combo-seen2, combo-seen1 
and combo-seen0 (Fig. 4d). These categories correspond to scenar -
ios in which both, one and neither individual perturbation is present  
in the training set, respectively, which increase the difficulty of  
achieving high accuracy 5,52. linearModel and scouter achieved the 
highest overall accuracy across all categories (Fig. 4d). Accurately 
predicting combined-perturbation effects fundamentally relies on 
correctly learning the interactions between genes. Therefore, we used 
the Norman dataset to assess the ability of current models to predict 
gene interactions
40. In this dataset, gene interactions are categorized 
into seven types, including additive, suppressor, synergy and other 
types (Supplementary Note 10). The additive-based linearModel and 
trainMean achieved the best performance on the additive interaction 
type, which is in line with expectations (Fig. 4e). Additionally, CPA 
performed best on the potentiation, epistasis and redundant gene 
interaction types, which may indicate that deep learning models have 
certain advantages in predicting specific types of gene interactions 
(Fig. 4f and Supplementary Fig. 25).
Among the combo-seen2, combo-seen1 and combo-seen0  
perturbation types, predicting the perturbation effects for combo-  
seen0 was the most challenging and placed greater demands on the 
model’s generalization ability, as both perturbations are unseen. 
In all four combined-perturbation datasets, the performance of all 
tools is the worst for combo-seen0 prediction (Fig. 4g). Notably, in 
the Schmidt dataset38, scGPT substantially outperformed the baseline 
model in terms of the MSE and PCC-delta metrics, especially for the 
combo-seen0 perturbation type (Fig. 4h and Supplementary Fig. 26). 
This may further demonstrate the superior generalizability of the 
foundation model in certain test scenarios.
We also assessed the scalability of algorithms and the robustness 
of the algorithms to technical noise and data sparsity using simulated 
data (Methods). The procedure and evaluation metrics were similar  
to those in the cellular context generalization scenario. In terms of  
scalability, none of the algorithms encountered out-of-memory  
issues, and their runtimes were within acceptable limits, except for 
scFoundation (Fig. 4i). All the algorithms’ performance was affected 
by data noise and sparsity, with greater sensitivity to sparsity (Fig. 4a,j). 
Owing to the long runtime of scFoundation, we did not evaluate 
its robustness.
Chemical perturbation setting.  For the three single-perturbation 
datasets, chemCPA achieved the best performance on the 
population-average metrics, outperforming the baseline model by 
a substantial margin (Fig. 5a). Unlike other deep learning models, 
chemCPA performed impressively even on smaller datasets, likely 
because of its effective use of prior knowledge during pretraining on 
bulk RNA-seq datasets 53, which enriched its understanding of gene 
expression patterns. In the combined-perturbation dataset, baseReg 
outperformed CPA on all six metrics, highlighting the current gap in 
methods for chemical perturbation prediction, particularly in combi-
natorial contexts (Fig. 5b). Therefore, developing more effective tools 
for such scenarios remains an urgent priority.
Next, we assessed the robustness and scalability of the evalu -
ated algorithms (Methods). The procedure and evaluation metrics 
are similar to those in the cellular context generalization scenario. 
In terms of scalability (Fig. 5c ), none of the algorithms encountered 
out-of-memory issues, and their runtimes were within acceptable 
limits. Regarding the impact of sparsity, PRnet and chemCPA were the 
most stable. Regarding the impact of noise, PRnet and cycleCDR were 
the most stable (Fig. 5a,d).
In summary, the following insights were obtained from the 
benchmark analysis of the perturbation generalization scenario:  
(1) T o predict genetic single-perturbation effects, GenePert is an 
optimal choic
e when the training dataset is small. However, when 
the fine-tuning dataset is sufficient, deep learning and foundation 
models such as CPA and scGPT are preferable. (2) In predicting genetic 
combined-perturbation effects, linearModel and scouter perform the 
best. (3) In chemical single-perturbation effect prediction, chemCPA  
is the preferred choice. (4) At present, methods for predicting  
chemical combined-perturbation effects are limited, and the baseReg 
baseline model achieves the highest accuracy.
