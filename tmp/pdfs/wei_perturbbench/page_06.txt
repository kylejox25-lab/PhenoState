In the o.o.d. scenario, trVAE, CellOT and inVAE achieved the 
best overall performance (Fig. 2b ). When selecting a method, users 
can consider the specific needs of their analysis. For example, if the 
objective is to minimize population-average metrics such as MSE,

Furthermore, we found that, in contrast to population-average metrics, 
all methods performed relatively poorly on population-distribution 
metrics, particularly on the Common-DEGs metric. For instance, in the 
Haber dataset, although the top-performing method achieved an aver-
age PCC-delta of 0.86, uniform manifold approximation and projection 
visualization revealed a substantial discrepancy between the predicted 
and ground-truth distributions (Supplementary Fig. 12). Furthermore, 
on average, only about 10% of the true most differentially expressed 
genes were recovered. We conduct a more in-depth discussion of the 
underlying reasons for this in the next section on the ‘perturbation 
generalization scenario’ .
In addition, among the 12 benchmark datasets used in this study, 
5 included additional perturbation metadata, such as time-point/dos-
age information, and these are classified as multi-condition datasets 
(Fig. 2c). Among the 14 methods tested, only biolord, scDisInFact and 
scVIDR utilized these additional metadata 16,18,21. Through uniform 
manifold approximation and projection visualization, calculation of sil-
houette score51 and multifactor analysis of variance (Fig. 2d, Extended 
Data Figs. 2 and 3, Supplementary Fig. 7and8, Supplementary Notes 4 
and5 and Supplementary Table 2), time-point/dosage covariates gen-
erally had a substantial effect on the cellular response. We evaluated 
the performance of biolord and scDisInFact on these datasets, as well 
as the performance of scVIDR on the TCDD and sciPlex3 dataset, to 
assess whether these tools were optimal for use with multi-condition 
datasets. However, biolord and scDisInFact did not outperform the 
tools that did not incorporate covariates on these datasets (Fig. 2c ). 
This may be because, although the time-point/dosage covariates 
provided useful information, the baseline performance of biolord 
and scDisInFact—without incorporating covariate information—was 
relatively suboptimal. As a result, despite performance improvements 
upon the inclusion of covariates, these models did not achieve top 
rankings. T o validate this, we calculated the baseline performance 
of biolord and scDisInFact on these datasets. In the comparison, the 
performance of biolord was generally better than that of its baseline 
model (Fig. 2e). This analysis indicated that incorporating covariate 
information improved the model’s performance. However, in some 
cases, scDisInFact did not improve from its baseline value (Fig. 2f ). 
This aligns with the primary design focus of scDisInFact, which is to 
address batch effects and integrate single-cell datasets rather than to 
predict perturbation effects

Next, we assessed the robustness of the perturbation effect predic-
tion algorithms to technical noise and data sparsity using simulated 
data50. As the noise and sparsity in the data increased, the perfor -
mance of all methods decreased markedly (Fig. 2g). We also noted 
that the performance of the algorithms deteriorated more severely for 
high-sparsity data, indicating that their performance is more sensitive 
to data sparsity (Fig. 2b). In terms of scalability (Supplementary Note 8), 
none of the algorithms encountered out-of-memory issues, and their 
runtimes were within acceptable limits, except for CellOT (Fig. 2h). 
The increased computational complexity of CellOT arises from its 
explicit modeling of cell-to-cell heterogeneity, and it currently does 
not support GPU acceleration.
In summary, the following insights are obtained regarding the 
benchmark analysis of the cellular context generalization scenario: 
(1) trVAE, CellOT and inVAE demonstrated the best overall perfor -
mance on single-condition datasets. For accurate prediction of DEGs, 
scPRAM is the optimal choice. Additionally, when the dataset includes 
perturbation dosage information, such as in chemical perturbation 
datasets, scVIDR is the best method to use. (2) Models that consider 
covariates affecting cellular responses provide more accurate pre -
dictions of single-cell perturbation effects. However, most existing 
tools do not incorporate covariates, and those that do are still far 
from being sufficiently accurate. Given the increasing availability 
of multi-condition data in the single-cell perturbation field, there 
is a pressing need to develop high-performing models tailored for 
multi-condition data, which can fully exploit covariate information to 
achieve improved prediction accuracy. (3) All the algorithms’ perfor-
mance was affected by data noise and sparsity, with greater sensitivity 
to sparsity. (4) In terms of scalability, none of the algorithms encoun-
tered out-of-memory issues, and their runtimes were within acceptable 
limits, except for CellOT.
Limitations of current methods in the cellular context 
generalization scenario
We observed substantial performance variability among the 14 algo-
rithms across the 12 datasets in the cellular context generalization 
test. In some datasets, all methods achieved excellent performance, 
whereas in others, the performance of all methods was notably poor. 
For example, in the Afriat dataset, even the trainMean baseline per-
formed exceptionally well, with a median PCC-delta of 0.92 and an 
average value of 0.85 (Fig. 2c). On the other hand, in datasets such 
as KaggleCrossCell, all machine learning models performed poorly, 
with a median PCC-delta of 0.54 and an average value of 0.52 (Fig. 2c).
T o investigate the underlying reasons for these discrepancies, we 
conducted the following analyses: (1) We compared the performance 
of the methods in the i.i.d. and o.o.d. scenarios. As mentioned earlier, 
in the i.i.d. scenario, the model was trained using the same training 
data as in the o.o.d. scenario but with additional access to half of the 
cells from the held-out context. In other words, in the i.i.d. scenario, 
we can assume that for each test context, the training dataset always 
includes a context that is highly similar to it. Therefore, the predic -
tion task in i.i.d. scenarios is simpler, while making predictions for 
the same cellular context in o.o.d scenarios is more challenging and 
places greater demands on the model’s generalization capabilities.
