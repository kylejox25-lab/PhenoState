Benchmarking analysis for the cellular context generalization 
scenario
In the cellular context generalization scenario, we evaluated the predic-
tion of known perturbations in previously unobserved cellular contexts. 
The cellular context generalization scenario was further divided into 
two distinct test settings by partitioning the training and test datasets 
into independent and identically distributed or in-distribution (i.i.d.) 
and out-of-distribution (o.o.d.) settings (Fig. 2a)14,18. In the o.o.d. set-
ting, we used leave-one-out cross-validation, where one context was 
held out as the test set, while the remaining contexts served as the 
training set (Supplementary Note 3). In the i.i.d. scenario, the model 
was trained using the same training data as in the o.o.d. scenario but 
with access to some of the cells from the held-out context. These extra 
training cells and the test cells were assumed to be independent and 
identically distributed14. In practical applications, the o.o.d. scenario 
is more commonly encountered, as we often aim to predict the effects 
of perturbations in a new, previously unseen context. Furthermore, 
the i.i.d. setting can be considered a special case of the o.o.d. scenario, 
where the test context has a highly similar counterpart in the training 
data. Therefore, in the following benchmark analysis, we focus primar-
ily on the o.o.d. setting scenario, and the results of the i.i.d. setting 
scenario can be found in Supplementary Fig. 1.
